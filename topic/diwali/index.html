Diwali

🌎How the world celebrates ↓

Diwali is a five-day season, not a single day. This page separates the main day (Lakshmi–Ganesha Puja) from the opening Dhanteras and the closing Bhai Dooj, and compares two very different statuses: a national holiday with a five-day ritual sequence in India, and a public-school closure in New York City that only became law in 2023.

        🇮🇳How does India mark Diwali? · Dhanteras

        The season opens with Dhanteras. The customary act is buying gold or new metal utensils and offering prayers at home, treating acquisition itself as a way of welcoming abundance. It usually falls two days before the main day, but days in the Hindu lunisolar calendar can compress, so the actual date follows local almanacs. What is bought, and how much, depends heavily on income and region. Since 2016 this day carries an additional official identity: the Indian government also designated it National Ayurveda Day, on the ground that it is the birth anniversary of Dhanvantari, the physician of the gods. Two things therefore sit on the same date — the household buying of metal and the lighting of lamps that continue as before, and a nationwide campaign run by the health authorities. A traditional festival being used as a public-health calendar slot happens on none of the other four days.

        utsav.gov.in ↗

        🇮🇳How does India mark Diwali? · Diwali / Deepavali

        The main day is the third of the five. Households light lamps and offer prayers to Ganesha and Lakshmi for prosperity. India's tourism ministry points to Ayodhya, Varanasi, Amritsar, Jaipur, and southern regions as places where the season is most visible, but how many lamps are lit, which deities are honoured, and whether there are fireworks vary a great deal between households. In 2025 Deepavali was inscribed on UNESCO's list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

        🇺🇸How does United States mark Diwali?Diwali

        Diwali is not a federal holiday in the United States, but New York State passed legislation in November 2023 closing New York City public schools on the day. Its meaning is therefore different from India's: not a national religious season, but one city's administrative recognition of who lives there. The closure follows the Indian calendar, so it lands on a different Gregorian date each year.

        🇮🇳How does India mark Diwali? · Govardhan Puja

        The fourth day of the season is Govardhan Puja. It commemorates Krishna lifting Mount Govardhan to shelter villagers from the rain, and is commonly kept with an annakut, an offering of food heaped into a hill, stacking the mountain and the idea of abundance into one image. The day shifts the weight of the five-day season from household wealth towards community and livestock — Diwali is not only lamps; a whole narrative of farming life sits in the middle of it.

        utsav.gov.in ↗

        🇮🇳How does India mark Diwali? · Bhai Dooj

        The season closes with Bhai Dooj, where the focus shifts from deities back to siblings: sisters perform the Tika ceremony and pray for their brothers' long life, and brothers give gifts in return. This keeps Diwali from being only about prosperity; it is also a renewal of kin obligation. Households without siblings, or communities that do not keep this rite, do not treat the season as incomplete. The last of the five days follows a different line of relation: not the household to wealth, nor the household to the gods, but sisters to brothers. The sister marks her brother's forehead and prepares food; the brother answers with a gift. This day explains why treating Diwali as a single evening goes wrong — each of the five days addresses a different party, and this one addresses the mutual keeping between siblings.

        🇧🇷Does Brazil have Diwali?

        There is no Diwali in Brazil. The nationwide holidays are set by Law 662 of 1949, which fixes seven civil dates, plus Law 6.802 of 1980, which adds 12 October for Our Lady of Aparecida. Religious days are handed by Law 9.093 of 1993 to the **municipality**: each may declare days of observance by municipal law according to local tradition, **no more than four**, and Good Friday already takes one of those slots. So in Brazil the question of whether a religious festival is a day off is answered not federally but by the town you live in — and there are only four slots to give.

        🇨🇳Does China have Diwali?

        There is no Diwali in China. The Measures on National Holidays and Commemorative Days split into days off for all citizens — New Year, Spring Festival, Qingming, Labour Day, Dragon Boat, Mid-Autumn, National Day — and days off for some — Women's Day, Youth Day, Children's Day, Army Day; neither class contains a religious festival. Religious days here are a **local** matter: Eid al-Adha and Eid al-Fitr are set as days off by the autonomous regions where the believing population is concentrated, never nationwide. The Hindu calendar does not appear even at that level.

        🇮🇩Does Indonesia have Diwali?

        Indonesia counts Hinduism among its officially recognised religions and gives it a national holiday — but that day is Nyepi, the Balinese Saka new year and the island's day of silence, not Diwali. So the situation here is not that a Hindu festival cannot get in; it is that **the one that got in is the Balinese one**: Indonesia's Hindu population is concentrated in Bali, the calendar and the festivals follow Bali, and the North Indian Diwali therefore does not appear on the national holiday list. Tamil communities in Medan and elsewhere still keep Deepavali in their own temples.

        🇯🇵Does Japan have Diwali?

        There is no Diwali in Japan. The national holidays number sixteen, listed in one article of the Act on National Holidays; the only two that touch on belief are the spring and autumn equinox days, and their statutory meanings are written as praising nature and remembering ancestors, not as any religion's festival. Put another way, Japan's holiday table keeps no slot for an imported religious festival — not just Diwali is missing; none of them is there.

        🇹🇼Does Taiwan have Diwali?

        There is no Diwali in Taiwan. The Act on Commemorative Days and Holidays enumerates the festivals, and the only one where lamps are the point is the Lantern Festival — the fifteenth of the first lunar month, at the end of winter, while Diwali falls at the end of autumn. Both are festivals of light, but one counts by the moon and the other by a different calendar, and they sit half a year apart. The only religious days in the Act are the Daoist day and the Buddha's birthday; the Hindu calendar is not on that list at all.

        law.moj.gov.tw ↗
