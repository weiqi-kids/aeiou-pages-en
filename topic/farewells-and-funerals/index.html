        🇧🇷Brazil

        Death registration in Brazil is handled under the Public Registries Act: the declaration of death comes first, then registration at the registry office, and only then may burial or cremation take place. The Catholic velório is usually held within twenty-four hours and followed directly by the trip to the cemetery, making the whole sequence faster than in most places here; the seventh-day Mass then carries the remembrance a week further out. Burial remains predominant, though cremation has been rising year by year in urban areas. The wake and the burial usually follow one another within twenty-four hours, so relatives come running rather than being invited.

        🇨🇳China

        In mainland China a death first affects the household registration: the Household Registration Regulations require the head of household, a relative, a guardian or a neighbour to report the death to the registration authority and cancel the registration. Cremation is the general rule in urban areas, with funeral homes and public cemeteries administered by the civil affairs authorities. Grave-tending around Qingming and the winter solstice is the most concentrated period of the year, and some cities add booking systems and caps on numbers. Because cremation and public cemeteries are the managed norm, the moment of burial is often set by the cemetery's own schedule rather than by a date chosen at home alone.

        🇮🇩Indonesia

        Death registration in Indonesia follows the population administration law: the death is reported to the local civil registration office, a death certificate is issued, and the family card is updated accordingly. Muslim communities generally bury as soon as possible, on the day of death or the next, with a clear sequence of washing, shrouding, the funeral prayer and burial. Christian, Hindu and Balinese communities follow their own practice, and the Balinese cremation ceremony may be held months or even years later. Because burial is meant to be swift, news of a death travels through the neighbourhood within hours, and the speed of the telling is itself part of the rite.

        🇮🇳India

        India works under the Registration of Births and Deaths Act 1969, which requires a death to be reported to the registrar within twenty-one days, handled through the Civil Registration System, with the death certificate being the precondition for dealing with property and insurance. The form of farewell varies by community: Hindu practice is largely cremation, with a sequence of rites and mourning running about thirteen days; Muslim and Christian communities bury, on a different timetable. Within a single city, two or three parallel sets of funeral venues and schedules operate side by side. In many communities the rite on the thirteenth day is the formal end of mourning, and only after it does the household reopen its door.

        crsorgi.gov.in ↗

        🇯🇵Japan

        Article 3 of Japan's Act on Graveyards and Burial provides that, apart from statutory exceptions, a body may not be buried or cremated until twenty-four hours after death — that waiting period is written into the law, originally to guard against a mistaken determination of death. In practice the sequence is obtaining the medical certificate of death, filing the death notification, receiving the cremation permit, and then holding the wake and the farewell ceremony. The infectious disease law creates exceptions for specified diseases, allowing cremation within the twenty-four hours. Each seventh day before the forty-ninth, along with the first and third memorial years, is itself a formal occasion for which relatives are notified.

        🇹🇼Taiwan

        In Taiwan a death must be registered within thirty days under the Household Registration Act: the death certificate comes first, then registration at the household registration office, and only then can the transcript needed for inheritance and for cancelling records be obtained. The funeral itself varies widely — Buddhist, Daoist, Catholic, Protestant and folk-religion practice each differ — and the sequence of the seventh day, the hundredth day, the first anniversary and the merging of the tablet is still followed by many families, though simplified farewell ceremonies are increasingly common. The weekly rites, the hundredth day, the first anniversary and the merging of the tablet each have their own moment, so a death is not a single day but a timetable stretched over a year or two.

        law.moj.gov.tw ↗

        🇺🇸United States

        In the United States the death certificate is issued by the vital records office of the state where the death occurred, with rules and procedures differing by state; families usually have to request certified copies from that state to deal with insurance, banks and the estate. The balance of burial and cremation, the regulation of funeral providers and the form of cemeteries are also state matters. Religious and ethnic background makes the form of farewell vary enormously — from a church memorial to the seven days of shiva to an entirely secular gathering. A memorial service and a funeral are often held separately: one for the body and the immediate family, another for the wider circle, and weeks can separate the two.

        usa.gov ↗
