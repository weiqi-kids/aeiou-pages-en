Moving Home and Housewarming

🌎How the world celebrates ↓

Compare moving home and housewarming in Taiwan, mainland China, Japan, the United States, Brazil, and India, from gifts and neighbours to purification, prayer, and new daily routines.

Quick answers

When is it?
There is no single verified fixed date here; timing varies by place, household, or personal choice.

        🇧🇷Brazil

        Brazil has no compulsory address registration: moving does not require reporting to the state, and there is no equivalent of a household register. What gets updated in practice are separate records — the polling place attached to the voter registration, the banks, the tax authority and assorted accounts. Instead, proof of residence is one of the documents most often demanded in daily life in Brazil: opening an account, signing a contract or registering for almost anything requires a recent utility bill. With no household register, the bill becomes the evidence of where you live. Renting usually requires a guarantor or guarantee insurance, and that hurdle often takes longer than finding the flat itself.

        🇨🇳China

        In mainland China, moving is bound up with the household registration. The Household Registration Regulations require a citizen leaving the area of their current registration to file a moving-out registration and obtain a transfer document, then file a moving-in registration on arriving at the new place of residence. The reach of where your registration sits is wide: school admission, eligibility to buy a home or a car, and where social insurance is handled all follow it. So 'moving the hukou' and 'moving house' are frequently not the same event in mainland China — the person arrives first, and the registration may not follow. The household registration and residence permit formalities often outlast the move itself, and a child's school place along with many services hang on that line.

        🇮🇩Indonesia

        In Indonesia, address registration is tied to the identity card and the family card: moving means obtaining a transfer letter and registering the move in, then updating the address on the KTP and the family card. Because the KTP address determines the polling station, the school catchment and where many public services are handled, a great many people in practice never transfer it — working in Jakarta while the KTP stays in the home region is common, which also means registered residence and actual residence do not line up in the statistics. After moving in it is common to invite the neighbourhood to eat together, which serves as registration and introduction at once.

        🇮🇳India

        India has no single nationwide residence registration, and moving does not mean reporting to one office. What actually needs updating is a scattered set of documents: the address on Aadhaar, the electoral roll, bank KYC, the driving licence, gas and electricity accounts. Because Aadhaar is used as the basis of identity by so many services, updating its address is often the first thing handled after a move. Renters are also frequently asked for a landlord's consent document before an address can be updated. The hour for entering the house is often calculated for the family, and boiling the first pot of milk until it overflows is the most common single step.

        🇯🇵Japan

        Moving in Japan means filing a moving-in notification: Article 22 of the Resident Basic Ledger Act requires anyone who has moved into a new municipality to notify the mayor within fourteen days of the move, giving name, address, date of the move, previous address and household head. Moving between municipalities also requires filing a moving-out notification first to obtain a transfer certificate. Failing to notify without good reason can bring a non-penal fine of up to fifty thousand yen — one of the few places here that attaches a penalty to not registering a move. Moving day is not only about boxes: the notice of leaving, the notice of arriving and every change of address hang on the same schedule.

        🇹🇼Taiwan

        Moving house in Taiwan means filing a change of residence: the Household Registration Act requires registrations to be filed within thirty days of the event, and moving in, moving out and a change of address all fall under that. What it affects is not just the door number — where you vote, which school catchment you fall in, health insurance and every official notice follow the household registration. So moving in Taiwan is also a relocation of your identity record, not only of your belongings. The auspicious day for entering a new home is usually read off the almanac, so the gap in price between a busy day and a quiet one for removal firms is considerable.

        law.moj.gov.tw ↗

        🇺🇸United States

        The United States has no statutory residence registration: moving does not require reporting to any government office. What actually has to be done is a series of separate updates — a change-of-address with the postal service to forward mail, a new driver's licence and vehicle registration, re-registering to vote, telling the tax authority and the banks. Each is its own counter, and there is no master record. So the administrative burden of moving in the United States is not 'one registration' but 'remembering whom to tell', and whichever one is missed usually surfaces months later. Mail forwarding can be filed online, and most services only discover the address has changed once a forwarded envelope reaches them.

        usa.gov ↗
