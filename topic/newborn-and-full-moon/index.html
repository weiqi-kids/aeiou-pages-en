        🇧🇷How does Brazil mark Newborns and the First Month?

        In Brazil, birth registration follows Article 50 of the Public Registries Act: it must be done within fifteen days at the registry of the place of birth or of the parents' residence, and the period extends to as much as three months for places more than thirty kilometres from the registry office. That distance clause writes the size of the country directly into the statute — within one country, a family that lives far away legally has more time. Registration is free of charge, and the certificate is the first document behind the taxpayer number, the vaccination booklet and access to social programmes.

        🇨🇳How does China mark Newborns and the First Month?

        Article 7 of mainland China's Household Registration Regulations requires that a birth be registered within one month, by the head of household, a relative, a guardian or a neighbour, at the registration authority for the infant's place of permanent residence. The regulation dates from 1958 and remains in force. The one-month deadline overlaps exactly with the full-month mark: the day of the month-old banquet is often also the day the household registration is due. Only with the registration does the hukou exist, and vaccination records, nursery places and school admission all have to be able to trace back to that entry.

        🇮🇩How does Indonesia mark Newborns and the First Month?

        Birth registration in Indonesia follows the population administration law, with the birth reported to the local civil registration office within sixty days to obtain the birth certificate. Sixty days is among the most generous of these seven places. The family milestones run on their own clock: aqiqah on the seventh day, with the shaving of the head and the naming usually around then — two timelines running in parallel rather than together. The birth certificate is the precondition for school, health coverage and social assistance, so the sixty-day window is not merely an administrative deadline.

        🇮🇳How does India mark Newborns and the First Month?

        India works under the Registration of Births and Deaths Act 1969, which requires a birth to be reported to the registrar within twenty-one days, with the Civil Registration System handling it online nationwide. Twenty-one days falls before the full-month mark, so the naming ceremony, namkaran, and the registration usually sit at different points in time — the legal milestone and the family milestone not coinciding is not unusual among these seven places. The birth certificate is the starting point for Aadhaar, school admission and every benefit, and without it the whole chain stalls.

        crsorgi.gov.in ↗nic.gov.in ↗

        🇯🇵How does Japan mark Newborns and the First Month?

        Japan's deadline is fourteen days: Article 49 of the Family Register Act requires the birth notification within fourteen days of the birth, or three months if the birth happened abroad. It is among the shortest of the seven places here, and the name has to be settled inside those fourteen days — the notification form requires it, so the time pressure on naming is set by law. The naming ceremony on the seventh night falls right in the middle of that window. The child enters the family register only once the notification is filed, and the health insurance card, the child allowance and the resident record all follow from that step.

        🇹🇼How does Taiwan mark Newborns and the First Month?

        In Taiwan, birth registration must be completed within sixty days under the Household Registration Act — thirty days is the general rule for household registrations, and birth is specifically given sixty. If the deadline passes the office must still accept the application, and it will send a written reminder to whoever should have filed. Those sixty days cover the full-month mark exactly: the month-old banquet, the first haircut, the oil rice and red eggs all happen while the registration window is still open. Only once the registration is complete does the household record exist, and health insurance, childcare allowances and every later application key off that entry.

        law.moj.gov.tw ↗

        🇺🇸How does United States mark Newborns and the First Month?

        The United States has no national deadline for birth registration: the certificate is issued by the vital records office of the state where the birth occurred, and the rules, fees and process are set state by state. In practice the hospital helps complete and file the paperwork, and parents later request certified copies from the state. So registration in the United States is usually part of a hospital process rather than a deadline parents must remember. Religious or cultural markers — a full-month gathering, naming, baptism — are arranged entirely separately. The Social Security number can only be obtained after the birth certificate, and that number is the precondition for almost everything that follows.

        usa.gov ↗
