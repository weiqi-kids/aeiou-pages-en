Ghosts, Ancestors, and Remembrance

🌎How the world celebrates ↓

This guide looks at Ghosts, Ancestors, and Remembrance through dates, relationships, and local differences. It does not assume that every household or community follows the same practice.

Quick answers

What is this topic?
This guide looks at Ghosts, Ancestors, and Remembrance through dates, relationships, and local differences. It does not assume that every household or community follows the same practice.
When is it?
Taiwan · August 13, 2026–September 10, 2026；Japan · August 13, 2026–August 16, 2026；China · August 27, 2026；United States · October 31, 2026；Brazil · November 2, 2026
How do places mark it?
Taiwan、Japan、China、United States、Brazil
What differs by place?
Taiwan：Taiwan's Ghost Month includes offerings, household tables, taboos, and in some places large events such as Qianggu. Families, religions, and neighbourhoods do not share one list of things to avoid. Japan：Obon is a time to welcome and see off ancestors. Returning home, tending graves, and local Bon odori are connected in some places, while dates and customs differ across Japan. China：Zhongyuan practices in China may include ancestor offerings, communal rites, incense, and local ceremonies. Like Ghost Month, it brings the dead and wandering spirits into view, but local religions and families observe it differently. United States：Halloween in the United States is known for costumes, trick-or-treating, pumpkins, and neighbourhood parties. Its present-day emphasis is seasonal play and social contact, not a Taiwanese-style offering rite. Brazil：On Dia de Finados, many people visit cemeteries, leave flowers, and pray or remember quietly. Families, churches, and local communities mark the day in their own ways.
