Homecoming and Reunion

🌎How the world celebrates ↓

This guide looks at Homecoming and Reunion through dates, relationships, and local differences. It does not assume that every household or community follows the same practice.

Quick answers

What is this topic?
This guide looks at Homecoming and Reunion through dates, relationships, and local differences. It does not assume that every household or community follows the same practice.
When is it?
Indonesia · March 9, 2027–March 10, 2027；Japan · August 13, 2027–August 16, 2027
How do places mark it?
Indonesia · Mudik / Idul Fitri · 伊斯蘭曆 Shawwal 月初一前後；Japan · お盆 · 多數地區 08-13 至 08-16，地方日期可能不同；Brazil；China；India；Taiwan；United States
What differs by place?
Indonesia：Mudik around Idul Fitri is one of Indonesia's major homecoming movements, alongside family visits and asking forgiveness. Not everyone can afford the journey or wants to make it. Japan：Obon is often a time to return home, welcome ancestors, and see them off. Bon odori and local events matter too, while dates and travel patterns differ by region. Brazil：In Brazil, people may travel home for Christmas, Réveillon, weddings, or holidays; long journeys make distance and cost part of reunion. China：In mainland China, returning home is often linked to Spring Festival, Qingming, and Mid-Autumn; city, work, and distance change the choice. India：In India, returning home may coincide with Diwali, weddings, or regional festivals; trains, roads, and city work shape reunions, and staying is also a choice. Taiwan：In Taiwan, returning home is often tied to Lunar New Year, Qingming, or family gatherings; tickets and work schedules shape plans, and not everyone travels. United States：In the United States, homecoming may mean Thanksgiving, Christmas, graduation, or a school tradition; distance and cost can make it brief or virtual.
